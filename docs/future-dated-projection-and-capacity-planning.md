# Future-Dated Staging, Confirm-or-Reverse Re-Journaling, and Capacity-Aware Projection

## The real, direct idea, in one paragraph

Every commitment this business makes — an invoice raised (AR or AP), a Sales Order or Purchase Order placed, a stock receipt or dispatch scheduled — should post to `journal`/`journal_line` immediately, through the exact, same, existing staging-table-plus-trigger mechanism this project already uses for everything else, but dated in the *future*: the date the commitment is expected to actually settle or deliver, not today. When the real event actually happens, the real, exact, final details (a price that changed, a quantity that arrived short and left the rest back-ordered) drive a real confirm-or-reverse-and-re-journal step: the provisional entry is reversed and a new, real entry posted with the real, final numbers, dated to when it genuinely happened. Two, real, direct benefits fall out of this for free once it exists: a genuine, live projected cash position (every future-dated entry already sitting in the ledger, not reconstructed from due dates after the fact), and a genuine, live projected capacity check (every future-dated stock receipt/dispatch already visible on the timeline, not just today's live count).

## Why this is a real, different design from what's already documented

`docs/cash-flow-and-growth-planning.md` (Layer 2) projects cash flow by *reading* real, already-existing data — invoice due dates, recurring journal entries — after a real transaction has already happened. This design inverts that: the projection isn't computed from history, it *is* the ledger, because the commitment itself was posted, future-dated, the moment it was made. This also means Layer 2's own forecast becomes a much simpler query once this exists — genuinely just "every real journal line dated after today," not a separate reconstruction.

`docs/stock-lifecycle-review-plan.md` confirms `placeOrder`/`fulfillOrder` already track commitment-vs-delivered via Qty/Value accounts — a real, proven mechanism, but a quantity-only one, with no future-dated *financial* entry and no reversal-and-re-journal step. This design doesn't replace that mechanism; it adds the financial-projection layer on top of it.

## The real, direct mechanics

**1. Staging still stages; only the target date changes.** Every existing staging table (`ar_sale_staging`, `ap_purchase_staging`, `po_staging`, `so_staging`, `stock_receipt_staging`, `stock_dispatch_staging`, and so on) keeps its own, real, established shape and its own, real posting trigger — this design does not touch that mechanism. What changes: the `transdate` a commitment posts under is the real, *expected* settlement/delivery date, not the staging date, whenever that expected date is known and is in the future. An invoice due in 30 days posts today, dated 30 days out; a Purchase Order with a 2-week expected delivery posts today, dated 2 weeks out.

**2. A real, new "provisional" marker, not a new table.** Per `no-new-tables`/`check-schema-table-count`, a provisional (not-yet-confirmed) journal entry is marked the same, real way every other flag in this schema is — a real, named tag on the journal entry itself (matching the existing `journal.recurrence` column's own role: a plain, direct column on the one, real, permanent `journal` table, not a separate table). A provisional entry's own, real reference stays traceable back to the staging reference that created it, exactly the way every other posted entry already is.

**3. Confirm-or-reverse-and-re-journal, at the real, actual event.** When the real delivery, dispatch, or payment genuinely happens:
   - If the real, final details exactly match the provisional entry (same price, same quantity, same date) — the provisional marker is simply cleared; the entry itself needed no correction at all.
   - If anything real differs (a price change, a partial delivery with the remainder back-ordered, an early or late settlement) — the provisional entry is reversed (a real, ordinary reversing entry, the exact, same mechanism already used elsewhere in this schema for corrections) and a new, real, final entry posted, dated to the real, actual event date, with the real, actual numbers. A partial delivery genuinely produces two real outcomes from one provisional entry: a real, confirmed entry for what arrived, and a new, real, still-provisional entry (re-dated to the new expected date) for what's still back-ordered — never a single, blended, approximate number standing in for both.

**4. Capacity becomes a real, projected timeline, not just a live count.** Per the still-open item in `docs/inventory-lifecycle-blow-by-blow.md` ("Storage capacity as an actively-tracked, double-entry counteritem — not built"): once a stock receipt or dispatch posts future-dated, a location's own real capacity account can be checked not just for "is there room right now" but "will there still be room on the date this is expected to arrive" — a real, meaningful difference for a business receiving several future orders against the same, finite shelf space.

**5. Budgeted sales/purchases join the same, real timeline.** Today, `Budget` is a separate, static ledger compared against `Actual` after the fact (`getTrialBalanceComparison`). Under this design, a real, budgeted future sale or purchase (not yet a genuine, real commitment — a real, planned one) can post the exact, same way, future-dated, into the `Budget` ledger specifically, so a projected cash position or capacity check can genuinely choose to include or exclude budgeted-but-not-yet-committed activity, rather than only ever seeing what's already, definitely committed.

## Real, open questions, deliberately not resolved here

- **Which staging tables need this first.** Not every commitment has an obviously "future" date today (a Cash Sale settles immediately, by definition) — a real, honest first pass should probably cover AR/AP invoices and Purchase/Sales Orders only, where a real, future settlement or delivery date already exists as a concept, before extending to stock receipt/dispatch.
- **How "provisional" is marked**, exactly — a new column on `journal` (matching `recurrence`'s own precedent) is the leading candidate, but not confirmed as the only option.
- **How a partial delivery's own "split into confirmed + still-provisional" step is actually triggered** — automatically, the moment a real, partial stock receipt is recorded, or as its own, separate, explicit reconciliation step a person runs deliberately.
- **Whether this changes `getProfitAndLossComparison`'s own real, existing semantics** at all, given P&L reports have historically excluded not-yet-realized commitments — a real, genuine design question this document doesn't presume an answer to.
